READING THE MAP

Data notes

What we measure

NDVI is a satellite index of vegetation greenness. The year views show the seasonal value. The change view subtracts the drought year’s NDVI from the wetter year’s NDVI (or comparison minus reference for custom years). Greenness also responds to factors such as irrigation, land use, and fire; the comparisons do not isolate the effect of drought.

How the sample was built

The 12 California GeoTIFFs were exported from NASA Terra MODIS MOD13A2.061. The export averaged good-quality observations with composite start dates in April–September, then aggregated to 5 km California Albers cells. We retained 16,933 cells with valid NDVI in all 12 years. Missing or fill values were excluded; valid negative values were kept.

Percentages count cells. Border cells are not weighted by the fraction inside California. The GeoTIFFs do not contain per-season observation counts.

Why these years?

California Department of Water Resources reports describe 2014 and 2021 as especially dry years during separate droughts. 2017 and 2023 were wetter years that brought statewide relief, though local impacts could persist.

These reports provide statewide context for choosing examples. The graphs and maps use only MODIS NDVI. Water years end in September; each NDVI value covers the April–September season of the named year.
